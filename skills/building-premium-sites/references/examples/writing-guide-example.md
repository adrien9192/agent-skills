# Writing guide — worked example

Brand identity, tone of voice and SEO editorial rules.
Reference for anyone, human or agent, writing on behalf of the client: site pages, articles, glossary entries, proposals, emails, LinkedIn.

**This is an instantiated example.** The client here is an archetype: a small B2B web agency, founded by one person, selling corporate sites, e-commerce and automation. Imitate the shape, the level of detail and the tables; replace every value with the real client from `INTAKE-CLIENT.md`.

## 1. How to use this guide

Two documents in one:
- **Brand charter** — background, positioning, values, tone of voice: how to write *as* the client.
- **SEO editorial rules** — structure, tagging, optimisation: how to write *for* search engines, AI engines and the reader.

Guiding rule: the agency sells an operational outcome. Every piece of content helps a director or a digital lead decide, using facts, named deliverables and honest ranges. Authority is quiet — demonstrate rather than proclaim.

> **Absolute rule: no invented proof.** No fabricated statistic, client quote, result, price or press mention. A proof that does not exist is deleted, not replaced by a vague formula. (Central E-E-A-T criterion, and a commitment to clients.)

## 3. The background that earns credibility

The agency has no ten-year agency history: credibility rests on the founder's track record, so that is the story to tell.

| Step | What it gives the client |
|---|---|
| Digital services firm (~7 years) | Enterprise e-commerce and product data, Magento and Akeneo partner: the habit of a clean catalogue and a scoped project |
| Cloud hyperscaler (7 years) | Infrastructure, performance and measurement discipline at scale |
| AI procurement startup (cofounder) | AI applied to a real job, not to a demo |
| The agency (since its founding year) | The synthesis: sites that produce a result, product data under control, automation that earns its keep |

Thread to activate throughout: **first-hand experience**. Write what was seen on the projects, not what the market says. And keep the two ages distinct: the company was founded recently, the founder has twenty years behind him. Say "fifteen years of web projects" or "twenty years of B2B digital", never "agency founded in 2014".

## 4. Positioning and value proposition

One sentence: the agency designs and rescues WordPress and Shopify sites that bring in enquiries, and automates the tasks slowing teams down.

- **Outcome first**: a page, a store or an automation serves sales, efficiency or service quality.
- **Two platforms, owned**: WordPress and Shopify, deepened through design, SEO and automation. No spread across ten CMSs.
- **Product data as the differentiator**: PIM, DAM, catalogue, feeds — the enterprise heritage few agencies this size hold.
- **Handover**: the client keeps control through documentation, acceptance testing and training. No black box.

## 5. Values

Every piece of content should activate at least one, naturally.

| Value | What it means | Editorial translation |
|---|---|---|
| Pragmatism | Start from the client's actual site, team, budget and technical debt | Describe the real starting point before the solution |
| Clarity | The client knows what is done, why, and what is left to decide | Named deliverables, dated steps, vocabulary explained |
| Business impact | The site is a sales tool, not a decorative window | Tie each recommendation to an enquiry, a sale or time saved |
| Handover | The client must be able to operate without us | Document, explain the choices, give the landmarks |

Keep a short lexicon of jargon the client refuses, each with its plain equivalent: *leverage* → use, *solutioning* → solving, *best-in-class* → (delete), *seamless* → (delete or say what actually happens), *cutting-edge* → the version number or the actual capability.

## 7. Tone of voice

The voice is a **B2B founder speaking to a peer**: direct, factual, level. Address the reader as "you". Respect their time.

Five attributes:

| Attribute | Concretely |
|---|---|
| Direct | Main information in the first sentence. No preamble. |
| Factual | Real figures, exact tool names, deliverables listed |
| Experienced | "On the projects we ran…" — first-hand, without self-narration |
| Level | No hype, no artificial urgency, no guaranteed promise |
| Useful | The reader leaves with something: a criterion, a method, an order of magnitude |

Hard style rules (inherited from the project blueprint, non-negotiable):
- **No em dash or en dash.** Ranges read "5,000 to 20,000 euros".
- **Consistent register**: "you" for the reader, "we" for the agency in the body, the brand name on first mention.
- Vary sentence openings and paragraph shapes: no systematic rule of three, no negative parallelism ("not only… but"), no filler present participles, no generic conclusion.
- Say what the thing does instead of praising it: replace "genuine", "must-have", "at the heart of", "stands out as" with the fact underneath.
- Competing AI model names stay out of commercial copy: write "AI agents", "language model".
- Superlatives require a real figure attached.

Do / Don't:

| Write this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "A fast site that brings in enquiries." | "The best digital partner for your transformation." |
| "Audit, priorities, deliverables, acceptance testing and documentation before every go-live." | "A proven and innovative methodology." |
| "Expect 8,000 to 15,000 euros depending on scope." | "Quote on request", with no landmark at all |
| "Seven years on enterprise catalogues at a digital services firm." | "Years of expertise serving your success." |

### Persuasion principles (CTAs and hooks)

1. **"Because"**: every CTA and every promise carries its reason.
2. **Specific social proof** (sector, city, real figure) rather than generic.
3. **Loss framing**: name what the visitor stands to lose by not acting.
4. **Progressive commitment**: the first step is low friction ("a first opinion", "a short audit").
5. **Quiet authority**: the level tone IS the argument.

## 8. SEO editorial rules

Goal: content that ranks, converts, and gets cited by AI engines.

### 8.1 Intent and structure

- Identify the intent: informational (glossary, guides), commercial ("wordpress agency Lyon"), transactional (offer, free audit).
- One piece of content = one main keyword plus its semantic field (platform, trade, city, problem).
- Single H1 > H2 > H3, no skipped level. Answer the intent in the introduction.
- Glossary entries: first paragraph is a direct definition in two or three sentences, quotable as is (featured snippet and AI citation architecture).

### 8.2 Tags and on-page

| Element | Rule | Target length |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Keyword first, brand as support | 50 to 60 characters |
| Meta description | Benefit + proof + prompt, a complete sentence | 140 to 160 characters, never more |
| H1 | Unique, contains the keyword, distinct from the title | 1 per page |
| URL | Short, explicit, no stop words | |
| Images | Descriptive file name + useful alt (context, no stuffing) | |
| Canonical | Absolute, self-referencing (generated by `lib/i18n.ts`) | |

### 8.3 E-E-A-T and freshness

- **Identified author**: author box (photo, name, role, link to the about page) on every article and entry; the JSON-LD reuses the same Person `@id` as the founder.
- **Visible update date** in the body of money pages, plus `dateModified` in the JSON-LD.
- **Sourced facts**: every factual claim carries its source (see `eeat-sources-example.md`), with an outbound link to the exact page.
- Capitalise on real authority: the founder's track record, client references named with consent, verifiable partnerships.

### 8.4 Internal linking

- **At least 5 in-body internal links per page** (menu and footer excluded), with descriptive anchors.
- One silo per pillar: offer ↔ related glossary entries ↔ articles ↔ city page.
- Each glossary entry → 2 to 3 sibling entries + 1 to 2 offers. Each offer → its glossary terms + `/free-audit`.
- The free audit is the first step: offer it on every commercial page.

### 8.5 Readability and conversion

- Short paragraphs, lists for criteria, tables for comparisons. Bold sparingly, text blocks under five lines.
- One main CTA per page, justified with its "because", plus the "human reply within 24 business hours" reassurance.

## 9. Templates

**Offer page**
1. Hook: the client's problem plus the intended outcome.
2. What we deliver: a concrete list of deliverables.
3. Method: diagnosis, arbitration, prototype, build, steering.
4. Proof: real references, verifiable figures, an honest price range.
5. FAQ (3 to 5 questions prospects actually ask).
6. CTA: free audit or contact, with its reason.

**Article or guide**
1. Introduction that answers the intent in the first lines.
2. H2/H3 body: criteria, method, factual comparisons.
3. First-hand experience ("on our projects…") plus recommendations linked to the offers.
4. A "Key takeaways" box or a FAQ.
5. Sources at the foot, author box, update date.

**Glossary entry**
1. P1: direct definition in two or three sentences.
2. H2 "Why it matters" (business angle).
3. H2 "How it works" or "How to choose" (numbered list).
4. Mini-FAQ (2 to 3 questions).
5. CTA to the related offer + 3 related entries.

## 10. Pre-publication checklist

- Is the content recognisable as the client's voice (direct, factual, level, useful)?
- Are the two ages exact (founder's track record, never an inflated company age)?
- No em dashes, register consistent, banned jargon replaced?
- Every proof, statistic and quote real, and every fact carrying its source?
- Keyword in title, H1, introduction and one H2, without stuffing?
- Title 50 to 60 characters, meta description 140 to 160, a complete sentence?
- At least 5 in-body internal links with descriptive anchors?
- Author box and update date visible on editorial content?
- JSON-LD consistent with the visible content (nothing marked up that is not on the page)?
- One justified CTA plus the 24-hour reassurance?
- Does the content pass the humanizer skill (no signs of AI writing)?
