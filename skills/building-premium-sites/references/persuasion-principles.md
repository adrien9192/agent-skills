# Persuasion principles applied to site copy

Source: video "Donnez-moi 15 minutes et je vous rendrai dangereusement persuasif" (Alec Henry, entrepreneurs.com). FR transcript analysed 2026-06-05.

## The 5 principles

1. **The word "because" (Langer, Harvard 1977)**
   Giving a reason raises compliance from 60 % to 94 %. The presence of a reason matters more than its content.
   → Every CTA and every important promise carries a "because" / a concrete reason.

2. **Specific social proof (Cialdini, Phoenix)**
   A close reference (same sector, same city, same profile) convinces 3 to 4 times more than a generic argument.
   → Proof points anchored in sector/city when that is honest. Statistics and client results are always real, never invented (project E-E-A-T rule).

3. **Gain / loss framing (Kahneman & Tversky)**
   Fear of losing is about twice as powerful as the desire to gain.
   → State what the visitor risks LOSING (enquiries, SEO traffic, time) before the benefit.

4. **Progressive commitment / foot in the door (Freedman & Fraser, Stanford)**
   A small yes leads to a big yes. Order matters: the big commitment comes last.
   → The CTA offers a low-friction first step (free opinion, short audit, 30 min), not "sign here".

5. **Quiet authority (Milgram)**
   Calm creates authority. Argue less, repeat steadily, let silence work.
   → Factual, level tone, zero hype or superlatives. Competence is shown, not shouted.

## Where it is applied on the site
- Homepage hero: loss-framed headline plus reassurance with a reason ("because").
- Homepage CTA block + contact page: reason + low-friction first step + level tone.
- Contact form: microcopy with a reason.
- Pillar and offer pages: CTA "first opinion" rather than "quote".
- City pages: CTA with a local reason.

## Guardrail
Principle 2 (specific proof) is powerful and dangerous: it must NEVER lead to inventing a figure, a sector or a client result. Real proof gets rephrased, never created.
